One ant, two rules, and a grid.

Press Play. For about ten thousand steps the ant seems to wander at random. Then it starts building a road, and it never stops.

Rule

Write one letter per colour: L turns left, R turns right, N goes straight, U turns around. On every cell the ant turns, paints the cell with the next colour, and steps forward.

What the ant does

The ant stands on a grid. If its cell is white, it turns right, paints the cell black, and steps forward. If its cell is black, it turns left, paints the cell white, and steps forward. That is the whole program.

For about ten thousand steps the result looks like noise. Then the ant starts building a diagonal road. It repeats the same 104 steps, and each time it has shifted two cells along each axis. It keeps going forever.

Chris Langton described the ant in 1986. It has been proven that its path never stays inside a bounded area. Whether every starting pattern ends in the road is still an open question.

Other rules make other ants. Pick one of the examples, or type your own rule, and see what it builds. More about the ant on Wikipedia.
